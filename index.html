📚 Stock Market Basics

A simple beginner's guide to understanding the stock market.

What is the Stock Market?

The stock market is a marketplace where shares of companies can be bought and sold. A share represents a small ownership interest in a company.

What is a Share?

A share represents a unit of ownership in a company. The value of a share can rise or fall based on many factors, including company performance and market conditions.

What are NSE and BSE?

NSE and BSE are major stock exchanges in India where securities are traded. NSE stands for National Stock Exchange and BSE stands for BSE Ltd.

What is a Demat Account?

A Demat account is used to hold securities such as shares in electronic form.

What is a Trading Account?

A trading account is used to place buy and sell orders for securities through a broker.

Why is Risk Important?

Stock-market investments involve risk. Prices can move up or down, so understanding risk before investing is important.

💡 Beginner Tip

Before investing, learn the basics, understand the risks and research the investment carefully. Do not invest only because someone promises a profit.

💰 Mutual Funds

A simple beginner's guide to understanding mutual funds.

What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from multiple investors and invests that money in securities according to the fund's investment objective.

How Does It Work?

A professional fund manager manages the fund according to its stated investment strategy. Investors receive units representing their investment.

What is NAV?

NAV means Net Asset Value. It represents the per-unit value of a mutual fund and changes as the value of the fund's underlying investments changes.

Types of Mutual Funds

Mutual funds can include equity, debt, hybrid and other categories, each having different objectives and levels of risk.

What is a SIP?

SIP is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals into a mutual fund. SIP does not guarantee profits or eliminate investment risk.

Understand the Risk

Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. Before investing, understand the fund's objective, risks, costs and relevant documents.

💡 Beginner Tip

Do not select a mutual fund only because of past returns. Understand the fund's objective, risk level, costs and whether it suits your own financial situation.

← Back to Learning Topics

🔄 SIP Basics

A simple beginner's guide to understanding Systematic Investment Plans.

What is SIP?

SIP means Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, commonly in a mutual fund.

How Does SIP Work?

You choose an amount and an investment frequency. The amount is then invested according to the selected mutual fund's rules.

Regular Investing

SIP allows investors to invest regularly instead of investing a large amount at one time.

Market Volatility

When market prices change, the number of units purchased through a fixed SIP amount can also change. SIP does not remove market risk.

Long-Term Approach

Many investors use SIP as part of a long-term investing approach. The appropriate time horizon depends on individual goals and circumstances.

Is SIP Guaranteed?

No. SIP is only an investment method. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks and returns are not guaranteed.

💡 Beginner Tip

Before starting a SIP, understand the mutual fund's objective, risk level, costs and investment strategy. Choose investments based on your own financial goals and risk tolerance.

📊 Trading Basics

A simple beginner's guide to understanding basic trading concepts.

What is Trading?

Trading involves buying and selling financial securities with the aim of benefiting from changes in their prices. Prices can move both up and down.

Delivery Trading

In delivery-based trading, securities purchased can be held in the investor's Demat account rather than being closed on the same trading day.

Intraday Trading

Intraday trading generally involves opening and closing a position during the same trading session. It can involve significant market risk.

Buy & Sell Orders

A buy order is used to purchase a security, while a sell order is used to sell a security. Orders are executed according to market conditions and order rules.

Market Order

A market order is generally placed to execute a trade at the best available price in the market. The exact execution price may vary.

Limit Order

A limit order specifies the maximum price for a buy or the minimum price for a sell. Execution is not guaranteed if the specified price is not available.

Stop-Loss

A stop-loss order can be used as a risk-management tool to help limit potential losses when a position moves against the trader.

Trading Risk

Trading involves the possibility of losing money. Short-term price movements can be unpredictable, so understanding risk is essential.

💡 Beginner Tip

Learn how orders work and understand the risks before placing a trade. Never assume that a particular trade will produce a profit.

🕯️ Charts & Candlesticks

A simple beginner's guide to understanding price charts and candlesticks.

What is a Price Chart?

A price chart shows how the price of a security has moved over a selected period of time.

What is a Candlestick?

A candlestick is a chart representation that can show the opening, highest, lowest and closing prices for a particular period.

Open & Close

The open is the starting price for the selected period, while the close is the ending price for that period.

High & Low

The high represents the highest traded price and the low represents the lowest traded price during the selected period.

Timeframes

Charts can be viewed using different timeframes such as 1 minute, 1 hour, 1 day or 1 week. The selected timeframe changes what each candle represents.

Trend

A trend describes the general direction in which price has been moving over a period. Trends can change as market conditions change.

Support & Resistance

Support and resistance are commonly used technical-analysis concepts that describe areas where price has historically shown reactions.

Charts Are Not Predictions

Past price patterns do not guarantee future results. Technical analysis should be understood as a tool, not a guarantee of market direction.

💡 Beginner Tip

Start by learning what each candle represents before studying advanced indicators or chart patterns.

🛡️ Risk Management

Learn the basic principles of managing risk in financial markets.

What is Risk?

Risk is the possibility that an investment or trade may not perform as expected and could result in a loss.

Why Risk Management Matters

Risk management helps investors and traders understand how much they could potentially lose before making a financial decision.

Diversification

Diversification means spreading investments across different securities or asset categories instead of relying on a single investment.

Position Size

Position size refers to the amount invested or traded in a particular security. Managing position size can help control exposure to a single investment.

Stop-Loss

A stop-loss can be used as a risk-management tool to limit a potential loss when a trade moves in an unfavorable direction.

Risk & Reward

Before a trade or investment, it is useful to consider the potential downside as well as the potential return. Higher potential returns can also involve higher risk.

Don't Invest Without Understanding

Understand the product, risks, costs and possible outcomes before investing. Avoid making decisions based only on tips or promises of guaranteed returns.

Protect Your Capital

Capital protection is an important part of responsible market participation. Never risk money you cannot afford to lose.

💡 Beginner Tip

There is no investment or trading strategy that completely removes market risk. Learn first, understand the downside and make decisions carefully.

🏢 Fundamental Analysis

A simple beginner's guide to understanding a company's fundamentals.

What is Fundamental Analysis?

Fundamental analysis involves studying a company's financial and business information to better understand its financial position and performance.

Revenue

Revenue is the income a company generates from its business activities during a particular period.

Profit

Profit is the amount remaining after a company deducts its expenses and other applicable costs from its revenue.

Debt

Debt represents money a company owes to lenders or other creditors. The level and nature of debt can be important when studying a company.

Earnings Per Share

EPS, or earnings per share, is a commonly used measure that relates a company's earnings to its outstanding shares.

Price-to-Earnings Ratio

The P/E ratio compares a company's share price with its earnings per share. It is one of several measures used in valuation analysis.

Cash Flow

Cash flow shows how money moves into and out of a business. It can provide useful information about a company's financial position.

Business & Industry

Understanding a company's business model, industry, competition and future challenges is also part of fundamental analysis.

💡 Beginner Tip

Do not judge a company using only one financial ratio or one number. Look at multiple factors and understand the company's business and risks.

📈 Technical Analysis

A simple beginner's guide to understanding technical analysis.

What is Technical Analysis?

Technical analysis studies price and trading-volume data to understand market patterns and price behaviour.

Price Trend

A trend describes the general direction in which the price of a security has been moving over a period of time.

Support

Support is an area where price has previously shown buying interest or a tendency to stop falling.

Resistance

Resistance is an area where price has previously shown selling interest or a tendency to stop rising.

Moving Average

A moving average is an indicator that calculates the average price over a selected number of periods.

Trading Volume

Volume represents the number of shares or contracts traded during a particular period.

Technical Indicators

Indicators such as RSI, MACD and moving averages are commonly used to study price and market momentum.

Chart Patterns

Chart patterns are formations seen on price charts that traders may study when analysing market behaviour.

💡 Beginner Tip

Technical analysis does not guarantee future price movements. Learn the concepts first and understand the risks before using technical indicators for trading decisions.

🧠 Investor Mindset

Learn the basic habits and mindset that can help you approach investing responsibly.

Patience

Investing often requires patience. Short-term market movements should not automatically change a well-researched long-term plan.

Discipline

Following a clear investment plan and avoiding emotional decisions can help maintain consistency.

Do Your Research

Understand the company, investment product, risks, costs and relevant information before making an investment decision.

Avoid Emotions

Fear and greed can influence financial decisions. Learning to evaluate information objectively is important.

Don't Follow Tips Blindly

Do not invest only because someone recommends a stock or promises a particular return. Understand the investment yourself.

Understand Your Risk

Different investments have different levels of risk. Consider your financial situation, goals and ability to handle losses.

Think Long Term

A long-term approach can help investors focus on their goals instead of reacting to every short-term market movement.

Keep Learning

Financial markets change over time. Continue learning about markets, investment products and risk management.

💡 Beginner Tip

A good investor mindset starts with learning, research, discipline and understanding risk. There are no guaranteed profits in the financial markets.
